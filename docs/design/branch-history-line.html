Two readings of the same graph

A true git fork puts the merge-base on the left: both ahead and behind grow to the right, because both are newer. A comparison spine puts the merge-base in the middle: behind on the left, ahead on the right. That second reading matches the chips you already ship (↑ ahead, ↓ behind) and the trigger’s head → base pair.

Designs 02, 03, and 05 use the fork. 01 and 04 use the spine. 06 names the fork in type, then lets the stacks do the counting.

00 · What ships today

Names, a file count, and +/-. Ahead and behind exist in the palette as chips. The shape of the comparison is invisible until you open it, and even then it is two numbers.

Ships Trigger + palette

01 · Trigger spine

The compare control grows a one-dimensional history. Merge-base sits in the middle. Drag is 1:1, release throws to the nearest commit. Caption updates from the live value, so grabbing mid-flight never jumps.

Counts only Subjects if you walk Always visible

Press a node or drag along the line. Flick past the end to feel the rubber band. ← → once the spine is focused.

02 · Lanes in the compare sheet

Open the palette and the fork is the first thing you see, anchored to the trigger. Two lanes only: this is a comparison, not a repo browser. Scrubbing a commit is a preview of that point on the range.

Revwalk on open Origin-aware sheet

Click the trigger. The sheet scales from that button. Click the dimmed stage or the trigger again to put it back on the same path.

03 · Press inspector

A deeper depth of the same object as 01. Hold the trigger and a glass module grows toward your pointer. Drag to scrub. Release with speed and the playhead keeps going. Tap without holding still means “open the palette.”

Pointer-down Velocity handoff Needs the walk

Press and hold the compare control, then drag. A quick click is a tap. Drag the inspector down to dismiss it along the path it entered.

04 · Filmstrip under the bar

History as a physical strip of cards, like years in Photos. The highlighted run is the diff. Flick, rubber-band, snap. Costs a row of chrome, so only if people actually scrub commits.

Revwalk Dedicated chrome

Drag the strip. A flick coasts, then settles on a card. Grab it while it is still moving.

05 · Workspace glyphs

Wayfinding across many comparisons. A 2-lane sparkline on each row answers “linear, diverged, or behind?” before you click. Press the glyph, not the row, to peek the fork from that origin.

Counts for the mark Walk on peek

Click a row to select it. Press the little fork on the right to grow a peek from that glyph.

06 · Merge-base story

One sentence and two stacks. The diamond is the fork. Ahead and behind are quantities you can open. Simplest to ship: peel the merge-base for subject and time, keep using the counts you already have.

Counts + merge-base peel Copy first

Tap a stack. It grows in place. Tap again to settle it. The hero pill is the merge-base.

What to build

Purpose over inventory. One always-on hint, one place for the full fork, glyphs once the hint exists.

First

01 spine + 06 story

The spine can be honest with counts alone (cluster when the walk is missing). The story needs a peel of the merge-base commit: subject, author, time. Both live next to the trigger you already have.

On palette open

02 two-lane sheet

Pay for a bounded revwalk only when the sheet opens (cap ~40). The fork is the missing map for picking a base. Same origin-aware sheet motion you already use.

Later

03 press, 05 glyphs

03 is 01 with a second depth, once the spine is loved. 05 is cheap wayfinding in the sidebar. 04 is the one to skip until someone asks to scrub history as a primary surface.
